The first is Anthropic's strategy for low-batch inference. When you're serving a single request rather than batching hundreds together, the bottleneck shifts from compute to memory bandwidth. The model weights need to move from GPU memory to the compute units, and for a single query that transfer dominates your latency. The second is OpenAI's distilled Spark model. Instead of optimizing how you run a big model, you train a smaller model to mimic the big one. Distillation takes the output distributions of a large teacher model and uses them as training signal for a compact student model. The student learns to approximate the teacher's behavior at a fraction of the parameter count. Fewer parameters means less memory, less compute, lower latency.

Both approaches have trade-offs. The low-batch optimization path keeps the full model's capability but requires careful engineering at the serving layer.
