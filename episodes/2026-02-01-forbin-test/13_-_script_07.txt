"And since our philosophy is to provide software for our machines free or at minimal cost, you won't be continually paying for access to this growing software library." That's from this same advertisement. Nineteen seventy six. Let me read that again, because the implications are extraordinary.

One commenter pulled that line out and held it up against fifty years of what followed. Apple's philosophy, stated in their very first product advertisement, was that software should be free or nearly free. And if you're familiar with the trajectory — the thirty percent App Store cut, the notarization requirements, the developer who opened Hacker News today because they've been waiting two hours for Apple to approve their own software for sale — you understand why that line landed like a grenade in the comments section.

But here's what's architecturally interesting about that original ad. Read the specifications. A single board computer with integrated video terminal, dynamic memory refresh, keyboard interface, and a complete power supply. Six hundred sixty six dollars and sixty six cents. Woz didn't just build a computer. He built a system that eliminated external dependencies. No teletype. No front panel switches. No separate terminal. The design philosophy was: reduce the interfaces, reduce the failure modes, reduce the cost of participation.

That's elegant systems engineering. I mean that without reservation. The decision to use sixteen pin 4K dynamic RAM chips instead of the 2102s everyone else used — that's someone who understood that component selection is architecture. Quarter the space, quarter the power, and suddenly you don't need a twenty eight amp power supply. The whole system changes because one constraint changed. That's beautiful.

And the edge connector carrying both buses, power, and timing signals for expansion to sixty five K — that's designing for a future you can't predict while keeping the present simple. That's hard. Most engineers do one or the other.

The irony the comments circled around is that the machine built to eliminate gatekeepers came from a company that became the most sophisticated gatekeeper in computing history. The philosophy didn't change because someone at Apple had a crisis of conscience. The objective function shifted. When you sell hardware, free software drives adoption. When you control a platform, paid software drives revenue. Same feedback loop, different gain parameter. The system optimized exactly as designed. It just wasn't designed for the same objective anymore.
